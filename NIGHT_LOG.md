## Ideas

### 1. One flat fee per gig (no rising schedule)
- **The idea.** Every gig is posted at a single fee and stays there. The fee changes at most once, at the deadline warning.
- **Why.** In the kit starter's week (fee +10%/h to 1.5 × typical, then to 2.5 ×), Monday's 186 bookings came a median 7 minutes after posting at 1.0 × typical. From Tuesday the median booked fee was exactly the cap, 1.5 ×, and from Friday 2.5 ×: drivers had learned that our fees rise, and waited. The starter paid €118.6k in fees on €71.3k revenue.
- **The effect.** Starter on `spread-1`: −€72,613, 45.5% on time. Same quotes with a flat 1.0 × fee (halal-01): fees €52k on 591 orders (fee × 1.15 of typical on average instead of × 1.67).

### 2. Sell above list, because typical fee ≈ 0.9 × list
- **The idea.** Quote a multiple of the list price well above 1.
- **Why.** Typical fee / list price is 0.90 on average (0.6 under 40 km). At list price a delivered car earns nothing. Dealers' win curve (8 weeks of random quotes, `explore1`): 85% at 1.05 ×, 50% at 1.3–1.4 ×, 10% at 1.8 ×.
- **The effect.** 1.45 × list with a flat 1.15 × fee and idea 3: €6,824/week on tune, against −€72,613 for the starter on its own seed and −€8,001 for 1.3 × list without selection (halal-01).

### 3. Only win cars that will be booked before the deadline warning
- **The idea.** A request is quoted only when the time from the dealer's answer to the last possible pickup is at least 8 hours; otherwise we decline (quote 4 × list).
- **Why.** `explore1`: a car booked before its deadline warning is on time 96% of the time; one that is not, 25%. At a flat 1.15–1.5 × typical, 90–100% of cars with ≥ 8 h of slack are booked before the warning, 77% with 5–8 h, ≤ 58% under 5 h.
- **The effect.** Tune seeds, same price (1.45 × list) and fee (flat 1.15 ×): with the rule €6,824/week, on-time 91.6% mean (83.9% worst), 5 of 8 weeks ranked; without it €6,640/week, 82.8% mean (68.3% worst), 3 of 8 ranked. (Rule here also refuses private pickups, trade-ins and premium cars.)

### 4. Learn each dealer's price level online, quote the price with the highest expected margin
- **The idea.** A dealer says yes with a chance that is logistic in log(price / list) around a level. The level is a sum of parts: the world, the day, the dealer, the pickup place, the delivery place (place parts start from a prior fitted on other weeks). Every yes and no moves each part it touched (one Kalman step). The quote is the price that maximises (price − cost) × P(yes), for the one option where that is highest.
- **Why.** With random quotes on 8 weeks (`explore1`) the price at which half the quotes win was 1.20–1.49 × list by world and 1.01–1.78 × by world and day (price war day, outage day). A fixed 1.45 × won 177 cars in one world and 485 in another, and missed the delivery minimum where it won most.
- **The effect.** Same fleet (`base1`), tune: €6,824 → €10,367 a week, ranked 5/8 → 7/8.

### 5. An on-time model picks each gig's fee, and its risk is part of the car's cost
- **The idea.** P(on time) is a logistic model of the fee, the hours until the last possible pickup, lane length, who lives near pickup and delivery, private stops, premium, trade-in and our own backlog (fitted by `tools/fleet_fit.py` on our weeks). The gig's one flat fee is the multiple of typical that minimises fee + P(late) × cost of a late order. The car's cost for pricing = that fee + expected refund + a shadow price on the delivery minimum that rises steeply once expected late orders pass 5%. This replaced the hard rules of idea 3 (no list of refused kinds, no minimum slack).
- **Why.** Booked before the deadline warning → 96% on time, else 25%; and more fee stops helping above ~1.15 × typical, so what a car costs is mostly its risk, not its fee.
- **The effect.** Same quotes (`base1`), tune: €6,824 → €9,613 a week, worst week 83.9% → 93.8% on time, ranked 5/8 → 8/8; average fee 1.16 × → 0.95 × typical.

### 6. Read the dealer's note with a small grammar
- **The idea.** note → clauses → words → cues (a vocabulary in Finnish, Swedish, English; one typo forgiven), clock times and days → one fact per clause; a correction replaces, a denial deletes. Facts become the gig's stop windows: no driver before ready or release, at a really closed site, after the note's deadline, or without plates or licence.
- **Why.** 41% of requests carry a note; the kit's keyword reader got 80% of notes right (50.7% precision / 40.4% recall on facts) and we paid 10.6 failed visits a week.
- **The effect.** Reader: 22,099 of 22,099 notes right on 50 labelled weeks, 3,539 of 3,539 on the hold weeks (first shot 3,538); with one more random typo per note 99.7%. On `base1`, tune: failed visits 10.6 → 0.0 a week, €6,824 → €7,661, late among orders with a note fact 19.2% → 12.1%.

### 7. Let the fleet's cost alone decide volume
- **The idea.** The quote maximises (price − cost) × P(yes) with no extra reserve on top of the fleet's cost.
- **Why.** After merging 4–6 every week was 96–99% on time: the market's reserve (30% of cost) and the fleet's risk price charged twice for the same scarcity. The same requests won at 207, 272 and 342 orders a week were booked before the warning 92.7%, 92.6% and 92.4% of the time: up to ~340 a week the drivers are not the limit.
- **The effect.** Merged system, tune: reserve 0.3 → €10,280 (worst week 96.2% on time); 0.15 → €11,881 (95.5%); 0 → €12,711 (94.4%). Hold at 0: €11,487, worst week 93.0%, 8/8 ranked.

### 8. Quote every option the dealer accepts, each at the same margin
- **The idea.** Find the option and price with the highest expected margin, then quote every other option at the price that leaves the same margin over its own cost.
- **Why.** Bisecting each Monday request's price threshold on 7 seeds (`tools/probe.py`, 2,515 options to within 0.5%): the thresholds of two options of one request are nearly independent (r ≈ 0.2; the slower one is 8.6% lower on average with sd 14.7%). Each offered option is a second chance to win.
- **The effect.** Tune, same fleet: €12,707 → €13,512 a week, worst week 94.4% → 95.0% on time, 343 → 349 orders.

### 9. Two cars in one gig
- **The idea.** A new one-car order stays out of the app for at most 30 minutes. If a second car starts within 30 km of where the first ends, with at most 5 hours of planned driving and windows a driver can meet, both go as one gig at 0.70 × the two typical fees. No driver in 60 minutes, or a deadline warning: the gig is withdrawn and the cars go alone.
- **Why.** The typical fee of a trade-in (out and back) is half the sum of two single typical fees: the trip home is half a driver's cost. In randomised tests pairs with ≤ 4 h of driving booked 85% at 0.70 × (median 4 minutes), all-in fees 0.89 × typical against 1.21 × for the same cars as two gigs; pairs over 6 h booked 29%.
- **The effect.** Merged system on tune: €13,337 → €13,770 a week, worst week 94.7% on time in both; cars delivered in a two-car gig: 0.71 × typical and 99.6% on time.

### 10. Refit the fleet on its own weeks; the delivery minimum is a barrier, not a toll
- **The idea.** The on-time model is refitted on weeks played by the merged system (a gig's own fee against the fee level of our other open gigs, an hour already waited, how many drivers know us). The price of a late order is next to nothing while the books expect far fewer late orders than we allow, and steep near that share. The world's own intercept is learned during the week.
- **Why.** The leaderboard: the top ten deliver 345–460 orders a week at 96–99% on time for about our margin per order; we delivered 214–384. Calibration on our weeks: 6.6% late realised against 7.5% predicted, the fastest option 6.1% against 8.1%; the model asked 0.48 × list more for the faster option, a dealer pays 0.12–0.15 ×. A stress run with prices 15% lower delivered 422 orders a week at 94.2% on time: the drivers were not the limit, our caution was.
- **The effect.** Tune: €13,770 → €14,973 a week, 342 → 357 orders, fee 0.98 × → 0.93 × typical, worst week 94.7% → 93.9%. Hold: €12,170, worst week 91.3% (see idea 11 for the fix).

### 11. A gig that sits gets its fee chosen again after six hours; pair a new car with a car that sits
- **The idea.** (a) A gig with no driver six hours after posting has its fee chosen again by the same model, once. (b) A new car whose pickup is near the delivery of a car that sits unbooked in the app is posted with it as one gig at 0.85 × both typical fees.
- **Why.** Almost all late orders were one tail: 26 orders a week sat a median 70 hours at the lowest fee and were raised only at the deadline warning, three hours before the last pickup; 52% of them arrived late. A car that sits also keeps the queue long, which makes every new car look risky and dear. In a randomised test a sitting car paired with a new one had a driver within 3 hours 69% of the time, against 35% left alone.
- **The effect.** Tune, with the on-time target at 96%: €14,935 (board pairs, no second look) → €16,838 a week with the second look; 350 → 438 orders; worst week 94.5% → 94.7%. Board pairs alone, target 95%: €14,987 → €15,882 on tune, but one hold week fell to 89.7%, so the target went to 96%.
- **The rule, and what it teaches the drivers.** A gig's fee is chosen once more after six hours in the app and once more when the last day before its last pickup begins (of the 47 orders a week that begin that day without a driver, 24% reach the deadline warning and end late: `tools/late_study.py`), each time at least one step up; then only a lost driver or the deadline warning changes it. Drivers do learn from it (`tools/waiting_study.py`, new one-car gigs booked at their first fee within 15 minutes, Monday to Friday): 73 / 71 / 65 / 67 / 48% without the six-hour look, 71 / 67 / 59 / 58 / 33% with it; a look after twelve hours instead: 73 / 69 / 61 / 62 / 40% at the same margin and on-time within what eight weeks resolve. The look pays for that five times over (+€1.9k a week), and more looks do not: see "Raising a fee again" below.

### 12. The on-time model knows the pickup side
- **The idea.** Five more signs in the on-time model: the week's drivers near the pickup and near the delivery (every driver of the roster, counted by how near they live: 1 at the place, a quarter at the app's 30 km), private delivery, premium as a class, and the typical fee as a share of the list price. `tools/fleet_fit.py --add` fits them with the rest of the model held and its mean chance kept, so they move risk between cars and leave the control's caution where it was. The target goes from 96% to 97%.
- **Why.** At level 8 the shortage differs from town to town, and the roster shows where the drivers live. On the weeks of idea 11, orders with ≤ 3 / 4–15 / 16–50 / > 50 drivers within 30 km of the pickup were late 12.6 / 10.0 / 6.5 / 3.3%; the model said 12% for all of them, so under the delivery minimum's price it still won cars from yards with two drivers nearby (five at once from one such yard in one week, all late). Private deliveries were late 18%, premium cars 12%. The typical fee has a noise of its own and our fee is a multiple of it: where it is low against the list price, our gig underpays.
- **The effect.** Same seeds, strides of 14 minutes. Tune: €16,277 → €17,421 a week, 444 → 467 orders, 19.1 → 17.9 late a week, worst week 94.1% both. Wide (16 weeks the fit never saw): €13,240 → €13,201, 17.8 → 14.6 late a week, on time 95.3% → 96.2%, worst week 92.6% → 93.5%, weeks under 93.5%: 2 → 0. Hold (8 weeks, played once): €13,472 → €13,753, 23.1 → 15.7 late a week, on time 93.9% → 95.7%; seven weeks end at 95.3–97.4% (92.2–96.2% before) and one, with a flu in the capital region, at 90.7% (91.5% before). Orders with no driver before the deadline warning that end late: 11.3 → 7.9 a week on wide. The target is the knob (24 tune and wide weeks, margin / mean on time): 96.5% €15,081 / 95.6%; 97% €14,608 / 96.2%; 97.5% €14,269 / 96.6%; before, at 96%: €14,252 / 95.4%. In our quotes the faster option now costs a median €11 more (same day over next day) and €7 (next day over three days), against €16 and €11 before and the €14.5 and €6.1 that dealers pay for it; we sell the fastest option in 57% of the two-option wins (49% before). Without strides, tune: €16,838 → €17,116, on time 95.4% → 96.5%. Restart test: the same week to the cent.

### 13. Weigh the week's two unannounced events
- **The idea.** A week has one price war day (the competitor 20% under its prices) and one outage day and region (its backup 30% over them, for cars that leave a town and the places within 80 km), both from 08:00 to 18:00 and never on one day: 5 × 4 × 30 possible weeks. Each answer weighs every one of them, a busy region more often, and the price is quoted against the mix that is left for today. The day part keeps two beliefs, one if today is the war day and one if not, so a war and a low calm day are weighed by what each asks of the day; its spread is that of a calm day (0.059; the 0.125 before held the events). All of it is a function of the ordered answers, like the rest of the market.
- **Why.** Every day of 24 of our own weeks bisected on forks of the played week at midnight (`tools/probe_week.py`, 24,127 thresholds within 2%): the outage is +0.26 log on the cars that leave its region and the war day 0.23 under the calm days, as the rules say, and calm days stray by 0.04. The day part followed a war after a median 18 requests (09:45) and an outage after 67 (13:30) or never: over that day our level was a median 14% under in the region and 6% over elsewhere.
- **The effect.** Exact replay of the 24 weeks at the fleet's cost (`python -m tools.surprises money`): +€202 a week (standard error 66), 19 of 24 weeks up; tune €13,930 → €14,082 (7 of 8), wide €11,158 → €11,384 (12 of 16). The choices (two beliefs, busy regions, the calm spread) were made on 20 of these weeks, +€238; the four probed afterwards gave +€19 (2 of 4 up). A market that is told both events at 08:00 earns +€300. The level is within 5% of the told one after a median 4 requests on a war day and 7 on an outage day, and its mean distance from the measured thresholds on the outage day went from 10% under to 1% over in the region and from 5% to 2% over elsewhere; from the answers alone the market names the war day in 24 of 24 weeks (sure after a median 5 answers of that day) and the outage day in 16 of the 19 where it reached enough requests to measure. It also foretells the answers of saved weeks better (mean log-likelihood −0.498 → −0.482 on tune, −0.481 → −0.470 on 16 wide weeks, 25 of 32 weeks better). Played weeks, same tree and seeds, strides of 14 minutes: tune €17,421 → €17,813 (worst week 94.1% → 94.2% on time, 467 → 469 orders), 16 wide weeks €13,201 → €13,536 (93.5% both, 383 → 390); tune without the stride €17,116 → €17,607 (worst week 94.2% → 93.4%, mean 96.5% → 96.2%, 457 → 463 orders). A single week moves by up to €4k either way, so the days themselves say more: orders won in the outage region on its day €49 → €60 each on tune and €47 → €57 on wide (price 1.63 → 1.77 × list), elsewhere on that day the share won 34% → 42% and 34% → 35%, war days and calm days as before. A restart rebuilds it (12 of 12 restarts the same desk, the same week to the cent). In a week without a war the replay still gains €66, without an outage it loses €2, without both €118: what it costs when the rules' week is not the week.

### 14. Weigh the week's flu
- **The idea.** One working day a week a third of the drivers stay at home, and nobody says which. It shows in that day's first hour, when they give back what they held for it. The books count, at each day's first event, the bookings held for a car that must be collected that day, and then how many of them come back before 01:00. The fleet turns the nights seen so far into the chance that a given day is the flu's: each night weighs every working day it could be (33% of such bookings come back on the flu's night, 3% on any other; Monday has no night to show it). The on-time model has that chance, for the last day on which the car can be collected, as one more sign, and only where it is above one day in five. So on a Thursday that has seen no such night, a car promised for Friday is known to be the riskier one; at 00:30 of the flu's day the gigs that came back have their fee chosen knowing it, and nothing is sold for collection that day.
- **Why.** `tools/night_study.py`, 24 weeks: on one night a week (19 of 24 weeks between Tuesday and Friday, never two in a week, and the same weekday in every version of a seed that we played) 22-57% of the bookings held for that day come back in its first hour; on every other night 2.7%. Every driver who gives back gives back all they held for the day, and 78% of them leave for no gig that day. It is not our doing: cars of a bulk order come back like the others (30.6% against 28.6%), drivers with two gigs for the day less often than those with one, and the yard, the fee and the day of the booking make no difference. Orders won before their last day end late 9.3% when that day is the flu's against 3.3-3.7% (Friday: 12.7% against 4.8%). A gig that came back and has a driver again by 10:00 is late 5 times in 57, a later one 30 in 53. Friday holds 50 such bookings at its midnight against 25-31 on the other days, so a week whose flu is Friday has 24.5 late orders against 18-20: that was the week at 90-92%.
- **The effect.** One week moves by a point of on-time between two plays of nearly the same policy, so each policy was played four times on the eight heaviest weeks (the same seed at strides of 12 to 15 minutes) and once on 17 more (`tools/replicates.py`, 49 samples a side). Against ideas 1-13: on time +0.38 point (se 0.20), 1.6 late orders a week fewer (se 0.9), margin -EUR 23 (se 232), 18 of 25 weeks up, the worst sample 91.4% -> 92.6%, samples under 93.5%: 5 -> 3. The seven weeks whose flu is Friday: +1.1 point (the declared test week of the hold set 92.3% -> 94.6%, four samples 93.2-96.0%; halal-02 94.0% -> 95.7%; halal-08 95.2% -> 97.2%). In the flu's own night (five Friday weeks, one sample each) the gigs that came back and were rebooked by 10:00 went at 1.30-1.43 x the typical fee where they had gone at 0.96-1.19 x; 62 of 151 had a driver again by 08:00 against 50 of 141, 34 ended late against 44, and of the cars that had sat without a driver into that morning 18 ended late against 28.
- **Its three forms.** The sign two-sided (a day known free of the flu counts as safer than average) and the week's own intercept measured with the flu apart: the Friday weeks +0.74, the others -0.3 (on the flu's day and after it we sold a fifth more, and those orders ended late twice as often): on time +0.03, margin +EUR 61. One-sided with the intercept still measured apart: margin +EUR 160 to +469, on time +0.0 to +0.2, and the one week whose flu is Thursday in the heavy set down 1.5 points in all four samples. One-sided with the intercept as it was (the flu's late cars count as a harder week, which brakes the day itself and the day after): the numbers above, and the least code.

## Did not work
- **Every knob of the final configuration, bracketed from both sides** (Saturday 17:00–17:35; 24 tune+wide weeks, two samples a side by `tools.replicates`, against `main` with fees 0.85 / 1.0 / 1.15 / 1.3 / 1.5, target 97%, look at 6 h, pairs held 30 min up to 5 h of driving). Fee floor lower: one more step at 0.75 × typical −€771 a week (se 222), at 0.70 × −€1,384 (se 294), on time unchanged: cheaper gigs sit, the queue grows, every new car looks riskier and we win less. Fee floor higher: 0.90 × −€47 (se 246), 0.95 × −€359, 1.00 × −€1,024. Target 96.5%: +€112 (se 176) for −0.51 point of on-time; 96%: +€534 for −1.19 points. Pairs held 60 min, up to 6 h: +€284 (se 304) for −0.57 point. Look at 12 h: −€342, −0.11 point. None adopted: the configuration sits at its optimum on each.
- **A finer fee grid.** Fees in steps of 0.05 × typical from 0.8 to 1.2 instead of 0.85 / 1.0 / 1.15 / 1.3 / 1.5, same model: 24 weeks, two samples a side (`tools.replicates`): margin −€624 a week (se 284), on time −0.23 point, 9 of 24 weeks up. The coarse steps act as a safety margin on the model's fee choice.
- **The night's drops: found, not solved by a rule (idea 14 weighs them instead).** 40% of all dropped cars (26 of 67 a week) lose their driver in the hour after midnight, when drivers decide whether they work that day, and 92% of those cars must be collected that same day. A gig dropped in daytime notifies again and is rebooked in a median 20 minutes at its old fee; one dropped at midnight notifies nobody (no driver reads a notification before 07:00) and waits seven hours; 14% of them end late: 3.6 of the 6.7 dropped-then-late cars a week. In a week with a flu one region's drivers do it together, and when that is the capital region on a Friday it is the whole gap between a 96% week and a 91% week (`tools/flu_study.py`: 16–25 drops in that hour, 22–33 orders, 10–14 of them late, in four of 32 weeks; the one week under 92% is one of them). Tried: when five or more orders lose their driver within an hour, the gigs due within a day reopen at the urgent fee: on 24 tune and wide weeks those cars were late 14% → 10% and 8% (two runs), the week's margin −€54 to −€116, the week's on-time +0.1 point; on the hold weeks nothing (3.8 → 3.9 late orders of that night a week, the flu week 90.7% → 90.0%): not taken. The urgent fee at 2 × instead of 1.6 ×: −€740 more for +0.1 point. A barrier that lets a car safer than the allowed share earn room (to dilute a week after its bad night): −€342, and Friday's volume in the flu week did not move, because with forty cars waiting the model calls nothing safe; measured against the week's share so far instead of the target: 38 more orders a week, −€490, the flu week 92.2%. One more step after every drop: nothing (most drops are daytime ones). What is left is a known risk: a flu in the capital region on Thursday night puts a week within a point of the minimum, and no fee, target or price we tried moves that week. A booking made before the car's last day is the weaker promise (`tools/promise_study.py`, one-car gigs): 15.7% of those made two days ahead or more and 18.0% of those made the day before are given back, against 4.8% of those made on the day itself; whether such cars should meet the drivers only on the morning of their day is the open experiment.
- **Meeting the drivers on the day.** A booking made the day before the car's last day is the weaker promise (18% given back against 5% of those made that morning), so: keep such a car out of the app until 07:00 of that day? Randomised inside nine worlds by the parity of the order's number (`tools/meet_study.py`; 160 such orders a week; the two halves agree in weeks without the rule, 5.4% and 5.5% late): the kept half ended late 11.1% against 7.0%, in the flu's night 30% against 15%, paid 1.00 x against 0.95 x the typical fee and had a driver at 09:00 in 76% of cases against 91%. A weak promise is far better than none: on the flu's day the kept cars all meet a third fewer drivers at once. And 07:00 is no meeting: a gig that came back in the night has 1.4-1.9 viewers in its first ten minutes of the morning against 1.0-1.8 for one that merely sat (`tools/morning_study.py`).
- **A crisis price, to dilute a week after its bad night.** A car safer than the week's own expected share of late orders pays nothing for the room it takes (the floor at zero kept, so nothing is sold under its cost): 14 weeks, 13 more orders a week, margin -EUR 481, on time 96.0% against 96.05%, the flu week 94.5% both. The barrier's charge is also a markup on cars that we mostly still win. The guess behind the idea did not hold either: with the price of a late order at EUR 3,000-4,757 on that week's Friday the model picked 0.87-0.95 x the typical fee for cars due on Monday, not 1.3-1.5 x (`tools/crisis_study.py`).
- **The census's silent day is not the night of give-backs.** A direct offer that pays nothing, read or not, names a silent working day in 7 of 20 weeks (`tools/census.py`); it is the night above only in two of them, and the three largest nights have no silent readers. We weigh the night: it is the one that takes our bookings.
- **The early look after twelve hours instead of six.** 24 weeks: +€447 a week at the same mean on-time (96.4% against 96.2%), and drivers wait a little less; but the three hardest weeks end 0.5–1.1 points lower (92.4% the worst). Six hours stays; it is a knob. **The model without the harm to the cars that wait** (`crowding` 0): fees 1.12 × the typical fee instead of 0.94 ×, 88 more orders, −€2,291 a week: the term stays.
- **Raising a fee again, later or higher.** Each alone on tune against idea 11, none beyond what eight weeks resolve (±€400, ±1.5 late a week): further looks once a day (+€236 without strides, −€140 with), every six hours (−€514), fee steps up to 2 × at the looks (−€58) or from the first posting (−€389), 1.5 × for every gig that begins its last day without a driver (−€588, on-time unchanged), one more step after a drop (+€235, dropped-then-late 4.8 → 4.5 a week). In weeks of random flat fees 2 × from the first minute was 98% on time, but a raise sends no notification: after a gig's first minutes a higher fee reaches too few drivers. The on-time model, asked at those moments, is calibrated (it says 22% late for a gig that begins its last day without a driver; 24% are) and still prefers one step: it was right.
- **Ending the pickup window a slow drive early.** 3.6 orders a week arrive late with a driver booked in time (median 0.4 h late). A window that ends 15% of the drive before the last minute: 3.6 → 2.6 without strides, 2.5 → 2.6 with, margin −€228 / −€18. Half of those drivers collected after the last possible minute because their earlier gig ran late; a window cannot stop that.
- **A week that starts at its allowance** (the barrier's price is €300 from the first order): −€1,080, worst week 93.0%. **No trade-ins at all:** +€120, noise (the model already wins one a week). **No learning of the world's intercept:** the same margin per on-time point on seven weeks, but the hard week (halal-02) falls from 94% to 92.5%: it stays.
- **Learning each town's shortage from the cars that sit.** Orders won while two or more cars of ours to the same delivery place had sat for twelve waking hours are late 7.6% against 3.6% (one wide week had ten long hauls to one town won on a Wednesday, six late, behind cars that had sat there since Monday). But they are 12 orders a week at €52 each: refusing them buys one late order less for about €680, and the target buys it for about €125 (96.5% → 97%: −€473 and −3.8 late orders a week). Announced snow is the same story (orders whose last day lies in a forecast are late 18.6%, six a week; as a sign of the model −0.48, a gain of 0.0008 in log-likelihood), and so are four more candidate signs (hours the pickup site is open until the last chance, days with a chance, lane length as a logarithm or in bands, how long a car has waited): 0.0007–0.0020 each against 0.029 for the five of idea 12.
- **More cars in pairs** (a new car waits 60 minutes instead of 30, pairs up to 6 hours of driving instead of 5): on 24 weeks +€829 a week and 46 more orders, on-time −0.5 points, and the week with a flu in the capital region falls to 90.8%. It buys volume as a lower target does (96.5% instead of 97%: +€473, −0.6 points), a little cheaper; left as a knob.
- **A better-calibrated on-time model.** Refitting so that predicted lateness matches realised (10.3% → 9.0% predicted against 6.0%) lost €2,266 a week on tune (€14,973 → €12,707): the calibrated model accepts more risk early in the week, lateness shows two days later, and the barrier then cuts volume for the rest of the week. The pessimism was doing the work of a safety margin.
- **One belief for the day while weighing the events.** The test was then sure of whichever it believed first, a war or a low calm day: in halal-08 a Wednesday 0.13 under Monday was taken for the war, and Friday's real war was left to the day part. Replay: +€171 a week (14 of 20 weeks up) against +€238 with a belief for each case, war day named right in 19 of 20 weeks against 20.
- **Wider day and pickup-today parts instead of the events.** Pickup-today spread 0.08 / 0.12 for 0.04: −€110 / −€223 a week in the replay; day spread 0.09–0.18: within €40. A Gaussian part wide enough for a 30% step chases every answer.
- **Finding the flu day in what we see anyway (the bookings given back show a night of their own: idea 14).** The number of drivers who view our gigs follows the gigs we post, and the drivers who book follow what we won (fewest on the war day): no day of a week stood out. What does show it is a count of our own (`tools/census.py`): a direct offer that pays nothing to every driver at the same minute of each day. The drivers who read on every other working day read today too, 97–100 in 100, except on one day in 7 of the 20 weeks counted, when 15–35% of them are silent, in every town (halal-01 Wed 28%, -05 Fri 15%, -17 Fri 24%, -18 Mon 35%, -22 Tue 16%, -25 Thu 15%, -28 Tue 28%); in the other 13 weeks no working day is above 12%. Only in halal-01 and halal-05 is that also the night on which most bookings are given back after midnight; the largest such nights (24–26 bookings on the Fridays of halal-02, -08 and -28) have no silent readers and follow a day of many bookings. Orders won on the seven silent days found a driver as fast as on other days in six of them (halal-05's Friday: a median 12 hours against 0.6) and were late 4.3% against 5.2% for the rest of their weeks.
- **Price precision.** Bisecting every Monday request's threshold on 24 seeds (`tools/probe.py`): our learner earns €2,103 a Monday, an oracle that knows every lane's level €2,065, an oracle that knows every request's own threshold €3,723. A request carries a factor of about 8% that nothing visible predicts; cross-world lane and dealer tables made the fit worse. No money left in the level, only in the menu of options.
- **Trust as an asset** (a euro from a dealer still building trust counts for more): 27 more orders a week, margin €12,707 → €12,682.
- **Pricing a car cheaper when a partner for it waits** (`Fleet.saving`): 44 more orders a week, −€209, worst week −1.8 points.
- **Direct offers.** 12.9k random offers on 8 weeks: 25% read, 0.6% accepted (1.4% at ≥ 1.15 × typical; 0 of 556 after a deadline warning; best class — a driver who holds a gig of ours ending within 30 km of the pickup — 4.4%). Targeted offers on every gig unbooked for 20 minutes: 75k offers, 85 yes; €6,723 vs €6,824 a week, on-time 91.3% vs 91.6%. Dropped.
- **Opening low.** Every gig at 0.6 × typical for 30 minutes, then 1.15 ×: 13–18% book low, fees −3.6%, but one point of on-time lost and a median wait of 33 instead of 3 minutes. Dropped.
- **Trade-ins at a higher fee.** Typical fee is only 0.535 × list for a trade-in (0.926 × for one car), yet they were on time 17% / 38% / 61% at ≤ 1.0 / 1.15–1.3 / 1.5–2.0 × typical (83 orders): not worth winning at any fee we tried.
- **One low fee for everything at high volume.** 0.85 × typical on ~550 orders a week: 70% booked before the warning, 76.7% on time, not ranked. Drivers choose among our own gigs: a higher fee buys priority more than capacity (a gig's own fee has 4 × the weight of the level of all our fees).
- Flat 1.0 × typical on everything we could win at 1.3 × list (halal-01): −€8,001, 57.7% on time. We won 591 cars; drivers booked ~150 a day.
